## Three concise verified engineering lessons
1. **FACE — Measurement quality is part of the measurement.** Facial asymmetry metrics need credible, trackable mouth movement, landmark visibility, valid frames, and pose/occlusion checks. A number from a poor frame should not be treated as meaningful. No clinically established abnormality threshold.
2. **ARM — Know what the sensor measures.** Browser-based IMU records phone orientation change, not direct arm muscle strength or anatomical angle. Device grip, wrist movement, baseline, signal freshness and posture affect interpretation. Reference-angle agreement and repeatability have not yet been established.
3. **SPEECH — Missing recognition is not necessarily impaired speech.** Browser permission/microphone/ASR readiness and transcript completion can cause missing or limited observations. Browser ASR may use outside vendor processing. No verified dysarthria measure or patient-labeled diagnostic threshold.

## Scientific guardrails
- Feasibility of obtaining some technically valid data demonstrated descriptively; repeatability/reproducibility across devices not yet established.
- Script uses `DEAD_MIC_DETECTED` only as app-derived technical label; not proof of a broken physical microphone.
- Face low quality does not necessarily mean pathology. Arm drift is a phone motion metric. ASR incompleteness is not dysarthria.
- No clinically labeled patient cohort, sensitivity, specificity, validated diagnosis/rule-out, emergency-decision time impact.
- Same-session repeated attempts and consent/build differences can confound apparent reliability.
- Public app uses browser ASR; do not state voice processing is exclusively local. Local Whisper is Presenter experiment until independently demonstrated.
- Preserve emergency instruction elsewhere in poster: suspected stroke warrants immediate emergency help rather than waiting for app.
